# Monthly Progress Report — September 2026

**Project:** MSc dissertation, University of Hull
**Working title:** *Learned Withdrawal and Evaluation Blindness in
Reinforcement Learning for Trading: A Corrected-Protocol Case Study*
**Purpose of this report:** explain, for a reader who has not followed
the technical audit, what changed since the pre-audit framing, why, and
what the dissertation now claims.

---

## 1. The research question moved

**What it was:** *Does machine-learning gating of trading strategies
improve risk-adjusted performance, and can a reinforcement-learning
agent do better than a supervised gating baseline?*

**What it is now:** *Why did the reinforcement-learning agent fail to
learn the task it was set — and why did the standard evaluation
methodology not detect that failure?*

The pivot was not a choice but a consequence of the evidence. When the
evaluation protocol was corrected, the original question turned out to
be unanswerable at the scale of the data — and the more interesting
finding was sitting underneath it: the agent had learned to do almost
nothing, and both the reward design and the evaluation methodology had
conspired to hide this.

## 2. Why the title changed

The original title — *Machine Learning Regime Dynamics in Multi-Asset
Execution* — used two reserved terms it could not support:

- **"Regime dynamics"** implies transition modelling (state-transition
  matrices, duration or hazard models). The system classifies the
  current regime; it models no dynamics.
- **"Execution"** in quantitative finance means order scheduling,
  market impact, and implementation shortfall. The system does none of
  these.

Beyond terminology, the title promised a verdict ("gating versus
reinforcement learning") above a paper that ultimately shows the
verdict cannot be rendered. The new title names what the dissertation
actually contains: the two findings and the method that established
them.

## 3. What the audit found — four items

1. **The learned-withdrawal diagnosis.** The RL agent deployed only
   4–5% of capital despite having access to 67–100%. It did not hit a
   size ceiling (it used 73–83% of its limit when active); it learned
   to abstain. The reward function penalises drawdown with a weight
   (λ=0.5) whose accumulated penalty rivals the entire profit term, so
   the rational response is near-total withdrawal. The decisive
   evidence: a permanently-flat policy outscores the trained agent in
   19 of 20 seed-fold-pair cells — the exception is the best seed in
   the sweep, winning by 0.014. Notably, the reward explicitly
   penalises underperforming buy-and-hold, so the agent withdrew
   *despite* an incentive designed to prevent withdrawal.

2. **Statistical underpowering.** At the realised sample size, the
   evaluation can only detect cumulative performance differences larger
   than 60–103%. Detecting the observed differences (9–12 points) would
   require 21–36 years of hourly data. Any "no significant difference"
   statement therefore documents the design's blindness, not parity —
   and the dissertation now says so.

3. **Seed variance dominates method differences.** Re-running the RL
   agent across five random seeds swings its performance within a
   single fold by up to 27 percentage points — more than the entire gap
   between the methods being compared. The original single-seed
   comparison was one draw from this distribution.

4. **Definitional artifacts that flipped conclusions.** Three
   definitions were wrong in ways that reversed findings: drawdown was
   computed additively rather than multiplicatively; the exposure-
   matched baselines matched on incompatible exposure definitions
   (reversing one pair's verdict from "survives" to "worst of the
   random distribution"); and a headline opportunity-cost figure
   counted overlapping windows, inflating it from a real −67 points to
   an impossible 2,243. Each correction is documented with its
   before/after figures; nothing was quietly rewritten.

## 4. What is claimed now, and what was withdrawn

**Claimed:** a well-evidenced negative result (no routing policy —
supervised or learned — beat passive buy-and-hold under the corrected
protocol); the learned-withdrawal diagnosis with its five supporting
diagnostics; the corrected evaluation protocol itself, including
power analysis, seed sensitivity, and exposure-matched baselines, as a
methodological contribution; and a cheap transferable diagnostic —
compare a trained policy against a permanently-flat policy before
believing it learned anything.

**Withdrawn:** the original positive headline numbers (Sharpe 1.85,
drawdown −0.4%); "return parity" as a finding; "PPO provides risk
control"; all single-seed comparisons; and every figure traced to the
pre-correction protocol. The full withdrawal list, with mechanisms and
before/after values, is the dissertation's methodology chapter (§3.6)
rather than an appendix apology.
