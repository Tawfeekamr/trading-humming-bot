# Monthly Progress Report — September 2026

**Project:** MSc dissertation, University of Hull
**Working title:** *Learned Withdrawal and Evaluation Blindness in
Reinforcement Learning for Trading: A Corrected-Protocol Case Study*
**Purpose of this report:** explain, for a reader who has not followed
the technical audit, what changed since the pre-audit framing, why, and
what the dissertation now claims.

---

## 1. The research question moved

**What it was:** *Does machine-learning gating of trading strategies
improve risk-adjusted performance, and can a reinforcement-learning
agent do better than a supervised gating baseline?*

**What it is now:** *Why did the reinforcement-learning agent fail to
learn the task it was set — and why did the standard evaluation
methodology not detect that failure?*

The new question emerged from inside the original one. Correcting the
evaluation protocol showed the original question was unanswerable at
the available sample size — and once that was established, the thing
that merited explanation was why: an agent that learned to abstain,
and an evaluation methodology that made the abstention invisible.

## 2. Why the title changed

The original title — *Machine Learning Regime Dynamics in Multi-Asset
Execution* — used two reserved terms it could not support:

- **"Regime dynamics"** implies transition modelling (state-transition
  matrices, duration or hazard models). The system classifies the
  current regime; it models no dynamics.
- **"Execution"** in quantitative finance means order scheduling,
  market impact, and implementation shortfall. The system does none of
  these.

Beyond terminology, the title promised a verdict ("gating versus
reinforcement learning") above a paper that ultimately shows the
verdict cannot be rendered. The new title names what the dissertation
actually contains: the two findings and the method that established
them.

## 3. What the audit found — five items

1. **The learned-withdrawal diagnosis.** The RL agent deployed only
   4–5% of capital despite no configuration ceiling binding (grid
   inventory was in fact uncapped — it had *more* capacity than first
   diagnosed and used less). The reward penalises drawdown (λ=0.5)
   with an accumulated penalty rivaling the entire profit term, and
   double-counts transaction costs. The decisive evidence: a
   permanently-flat policy outscores the trained agent in 19 of 20
   seed-fold-pair cells — the exception is the best seed in the sweep,
   winning by 0.014. Note the reward explicitly penalises
   underperforming buy-and-hold, so the agent withdrew *despite* an
   incentive designed to prevent withdrawal. The finding is scoped to
   this unusually punitive reward, not a claim about RL generally.

2. **Statistical underpowering.** At the realised sample size, the
   evaluation can only detect compounded cumulative differences larger
   than 82–179%. Detecting the observed differences (9–12 points)
   would require 21–36 years of hourly data — and accounting for fold
   clustering, the effective sample is six policy refits. (The
   previously reported 'achieved power' percentages were retracted as
   retrospective power — a deterministic restatement of the p-value,
   per Hoenig & Heisey 2001.) Any "no significant difference" statement
   documents the design's blindness, not parity.

3. **Seed variance dominates method differences.** Five seeds swing
   the agent's performance within a single fold by up to 27
   percentage points — more than the entire between-method gap. The
   main run's seed (42) was fixed in code before any result was
   observed and sits at the unfavourable end of the seed distribution
   in three of its four cells: the headline figures are pessimistic
   toward the RL agent, not flattering.

4. **Definitional artifacts that flipped conclusions** — including
   returns reported as arithmetic sums rather than compounded equity
   (which flipped the sign of BNB buy-and-hold from +3.8% to −4.3%),
   drawdown computed on an additive equity curve, exposure-matched
   baselines matched on incompatible exposure definitions, and an
   opportunity-cost figure that counted overlapping windows 24-fold.

5. **Independent review caught what self-audit could not.** After five
   self-directed corrective batches, an independent review found two
   further invalidating defects (the return definition above, and the
   pooling of non-adjacent test windows — which had inflated a
   headline drawdown figure five-fold, on the strength of a contiguity
   claim the correction record itself had asserted and four passes had
   accepted). This is not an embarrassment to be minimised; it is
   evidence for the dissertation's own thesis. The shared false
   premise was invisible to every self-directed pass precisely because
   it was a premise, not a computation.

## 4. What is claimed now, and what was withdrawn

**Claimed:** a well-evidenced negative result (under compounded
returns, no routing policy beat passive buy-and-hold on either asset —
on BNB passive exposure itself lost 4.3%, and both policies lost far
more); the learned-withdrawal diagnosis with its supporting
diagnostics, scoped to the reward under study; one statistically
significant but exposure-explained result (PPO's per-fold drawdowns
are consistently below the supervised baseline's on ETH, consistent
with its 4.9x-lower capital deployment — and it still loses to random
entries matched on the same exposure); and the corrected evaluation
protocol as a documented case study (see §5).

**Withdrawn:** the original positive headline numbers; "return
parity"; "PPO provides risk control" as a skill claim; all
single-seed comparisons; every pre-correction figure; and the pooled
drawdown statistics. Each withdrawal is documented with its mechanism
and before/after values in the methodology chapter (§3.6) rather than
an appendix apology.

**Three distinct cost figures, often conflated:** the retracted
2,243pp false-DANGER "opportunity cost" (an overlap-counting artifact,
impossible against a +38% buy-and-hold window); the per-block
de-overlapped gap (0.96pp/0.72pp per 24-bar block, DANGER-flagged vs
not); and the realised portfolio-level gap of the gated strategy
versus buy-and-hold over the same window (−67.1pp ETH, −20.8pp BNB) —
related quantities, not versions of one another.

**What generalises (the transferable diagnostics):** compare any
trained policy against permanent abstention before believing it
learned anything; decompose the reward into terms; match baselines on
capital-weighted exposure, not time-in-market; never concatenate
non-adjacent test windows; report the minimum detectable effect with
every null; never report a single-seed RL result. Each is standard
practice in an adjacent literature; the contribution is the documented
case where each one's absence changed a conclusion.
